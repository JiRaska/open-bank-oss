# Threat model - openbank-aml-service

## Scope

AML screening cases, post-booking transaction monitoring and the suspicious-transaction report (ADR-0371). The service books nothing and sits in no payment path. Its assets are the confidentiality of who is being monitored or reported, the integrity of who decided what, and the completeness of what monitoring saw. STRIDE categories are named per threat.

Trust boundaries: Kafka in (`openbank.party.events`, `openbank.ledger.journal.posted`, `openbank.transactions.transaction.initiated`), Kafka out (`openbank.aml.events`, `openbank.oversight-triggers`), REST in from the operator console and from the payment services that open cases, REST out to account-service and party-service, and the service's own Postgres.

## Tipping-off (information disclosure)

Telling a customer that they are monitored or reported is the worst outcome here. A leak cannot be recalled.

- **A monitoring case or a report reaches a customer-facing surface.** The report and disposition endpoints (`AmlReportResource`, `AmlMonitoringDispositionResource`) admit `ROLE_COMPLIANCE` only, in `@RolesAllowed` and in `aml_rest_ext.rego` (`compliance-aml-monitoring-and-report`, human principals only). `AmlCaseResource` answers 404 for a pack-monitoring case to every caller without that role and drops such cases from its list, so the existing case API does not reveal that one exists. A pack-monitoring case is one the evaluation job opened; it is told apart from a `TRANSACTION_MONITORING` case a payment rail opened by a reserved idempotency-key prefix that the create endpoint refuses, so no caller can mint or disguise one. Accepted limitation: rail-opened cases keep their existing audience and single-analyst decision. No customer-edge or notification contract carries these types. Gap: nothing mechanical stops a future contract from adding one; the planned gate `aml-no-customer-surface` is not built.
- **An operator reads a report through the fleet-wide read grant.** The base policy grants every operator any action ending in `.read` or `.list`. The report and disposition read actions are named `amlReport.inspect` and `amlMonitoring.inspect` so they fall outside that grant; the rego test file holds the negative control that shows a `.read` name would have leaked.
- **Event fan-out.** `AmlReportStateChangedEvent` carries a report id, a state and an instant, and nothing else, because `openbank.aml.events` is read by other services. Accepted limitation: `AmlCaseCreatedEvent` and `AmlCaseStatusChangedEvent` still carry the party id and the alert code for every case type, and party-service, domestic-payment and context-service consume that topic. While evaluation runs in SHADOW no monitoring case is created, so nothing is emitted; this must be closed before case opening is switched on (`openbank.aml.monitoring.evaluation.open-cases`).
- **A machine principal reads monitoring data.** Machine principals are refused by the policy rule even when they hold the compliance role. Accepted limitation: agent-service reads cases through `amlCase.read`; the 404 rule above applies to it, so it sees no monitoring case.
- **Logs and metrics.** Monitoring log lines carry counts, ids of packs and scenario codes on metric tags, never a party id on a metric. Case and report ids appear in logs; the narrative and the filing reference do not.
- **No model call.** The service calls no language model. Any assistance runs in the agent plane under its own controls (ADR-0371).

## Decisions (spoofing, repudiation, elevation of privilege)

- **A caller names someone else as the decider (spoofing).** `UpdateAmlDecisionRequest.decidedBy` is ignored; `AmlCaseResource` takes the decider from the authenticated principal. The same holds for every proposer, approver, drafter and filing officer.
- **One person closes a monitoring case (elevation of privilege).** `AmlCaseService` refuses to close or escalate a pack-monitoring case without an approved proposal, so the plain decision endpoint, the sandbox auto-clear (`OnboardingScreening`) and any future job cannot do it. `MonitoringDispositionService` uses the fleet maker-checker primitive, where the approver must differ from the proposer, and `aml_case_disposition_proposal` repeats the rule as a CHECK constraint. Accepted limitation: two accounts held by one person defeat any four-eyes control; that is an identity-governance matter outside this service.
- **A report is filed without a second approver.** `SuspiciousTransactionReport` reaches FILED only from APPROVED, and approval refuses the drafter. The table `aml_str` refuses FILED without an approver, a reference, an instant and a recording officer, and refuses an approver equal to the drafter, whatever the application does.
- **A filing that did not happen (repudiation).** FILED records that a named officer filed the report outside this system, under a reference. The service submits nothing and claims no electronic submission. Accepted limitation: the authenticity of the reference rests on that officer.
- **A decision without a reason anyone can learn from.** Every closing decision takes a code from the versioned outcome vocabulary (`DecisionOutcomes`); an unknown code is refused. The stored label pins the vocabulary revision and hash.
- **Concurrent decisions.** Proposal and report transitions are compare-and-set on the previous state; the loser changes nothing and gets a conflict.

## Monitoring intake and evaluation (tampering, denial of service)

- **A forged or malformed event inflates or hides counts (tampering).** Only principals with a Kafka write ACL on the ledger and transaction topics can publish there. `LedgerBookedMovementConsumer` rejects incomplete or implausible bookings to its dead-letter topic rather than acking them. Accepted limitation: the payload is not signed, so a compromised producer is trusted.
- **A replay double-counts.** A booking is recorded once on its business key (journal entry, account, currency); the claim and the counter increments share one transaction in `MovementIntakeRepositoryImpl`.
- **Monitoring silently sees nothing (denial of service).** Each consumer has its own dead-letter topic; `MovementAttributionScheduler` publishes the uncounted backlog and its age; `MonitoringEvaluationScheduler` records workflow liveness only for a tick that reached every active party. An evaluation that could not run is stored as INCOMPLETE with its reason, never as "nothing found".
- **A pack or scheme is swapped (tampering).** Counter scheme, features, vocabularies and pack are compiled at startup and pinned by content hash (`MonitoringPackLoader`); a mismatch stops the boot. Accepted limitation: the shipped files are synthetic and unsigned. Signature, approval and activation belong to the ADR-0354 pack lifecycle, and case opening stays off until then.
- **Account-service unavailable.** Intake never calls it. Attribution retries; unresolved movements stay uncounted and visible.

## Held-payment case intake (ADR-0374 D13, tampering, elevation of privilege, denial of service)

`PaymentHeldConsumer` (channel `payment-held-in`, off by default) reads the event topic of each payment rail that has adopted the admission chain, today `openbank.domestic.payment.events`, and opens the one case of a held payment from the rail's `PAYMENT_HELD` event. This is a new inbound trust boundary: a rail can now create a case without calling the case endpoint.

- **A producer forges a case (tampering, elevation of privilege).** Only the rail's own Kafka identity may write its topic. The consumer does not take the case key from the event: it derives `payment-hold:<rail>:<paymentId>` and refuses an event that claims any other key, so an event cannot create a case under a pack-monitoring key (`tm:`) or under the key of another payment. The case carries alert code `PAYMENT_HELD`; a rail releases a hold only on a decision of the case with exactly its derived key. Accepted limitation: the payload is not signed, so a compromised rail identity is trusted for cases about that rail's own payments.
- **A caller plants the hold's case (spoofing, elevation of privilege).** The case under `payment-hold:<rail>:<paymentId>` is the one whose decision a rail releases on, and a key says nothing about who wrote the row under it. A caller of the create endpoint (staff, or one of the named machine principals) could open a benign-looking case under that key ahead of the hold; the intake then answered it as its own, the real evidence never reached a reviewer, and a CLEARED decision on the planted case released the payment. Provenance is now data: every case records a server-set `origin` that no request can set. The create endpoint refuses the `payment-hold:` namespace, in one place. The intake accepts under a hold key only a case of origin `PAYMENT_HOLD_INTAKE`; a row of any other origin found there is not displaced: the hold event is dead-lettered and counted by `openbank_aml_payment_held_foreign_key_claims_total`, and the payment stays held until it expires into rejection (fail closed). A case under a hold key with any other origin cannot be decided, so no status event leaves under that key from it. Residual: a decision published under a hold key before this control existed cannot be recalled.
- **A hold loses its case (denial of service, repudiation).** The rail writes the event in the transaction that holds the payment, and this consumer is idempotent on the case key, so redelivery opens nothing twice. Opening the case depends on nothing but this service's own database. A rail that could not read the debtor's standing sends no party, usually because the account authority is down; the intake used to ask that same authority, fail, and dead-letter the hold, leaving the payment held with no case until it expired. The case is now opened with `party_id` NULL and the party-resolution sweep fills it in from the account, least recently attempted first so an account that never resolves cannot starve the rest. An event that cannot be read, one without a debtor account and a failed write are rethrown to `openbank.dlq.aml.payment-held-in`.
- **A decision takes party-level effect on nobody (tampering, fail-open).** A case with no established party can still be decided, because the payment waits on it. Its events name a party only from one property, the established party or null; never a placeholder and never the account id standing in for it. The rail binds the decision by the case key and is unaffected. A consumer that acts per party ignores an event that names none, and when the sweep resolves the party it restates a BLOCKED case's status with that party in the same transaction, so a BLOCK reaches the real party then. The restatement asks one question, `AmlCase.partyEstablished` (party id present and different from the account id), so it also covers a legacy case that carried the debtor account id as its party: party-service looks the party up by that id and finds none, so that BLOCK never reached the party on main either. Only BLOCKED is restated (a restated CLEARED could re-activate a party another case blocked), and the resolution written in the same transaction makes it happen at most once. Decision and resolution lock the case row; a decision built on a read from before the resolution is refused. Residual: between the decision and the resolution the payment is blocked and the party is not; a case whose account never resolves has no party-level effect; context-service must be deployed before aml-service, because a pod from before this change dead-letters an event with a null party.
- **Erasure misses a case that does not name its party yet (information disclosure).** Erasure by party matched cases by party id, so a case still waiting for its party kept its reference, alert detail and matched entity, and the sweep later attached it to the erased party as it was. The erasure is recorded and the sweep applies the same anonymisation in the transaction that attaches the case; a per-party advisory lock serialises the two. The retention rule is unchanged: the row stays, the three fields are cleared. Residual: an unresolved case keeps its data until it resolves, and such a case names no party.
- **A hold floods the review queue.** A rail requests a case for an unavailable control only after its own bounded re-evaluation, so an outage of minutes opens no case. An outage longer than that opens one case per payment submitted in it; the queue's capacity is the operational limit ADR-0374 names.
- **Information disclosure.** The event carries no name. The case stores the party id, the account reference and, as alert detail, the reason codes and the evidence of the controls: statuses, scores and a matched list entry.
- **One analyst releases a held payment (elevation of privilege).** A case of origin `PAYMENT_HOLD_INTAKE` reaches `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED` only through a proposal by one analyst and a confirmation by a different one that restates the proposal's outcome (CLEAR or BLOCK) and reason (ADR-0374 point 3). The rule is in the `AmlCase` aggregate: `transitionTo` refuses those two statuses on such a case, so `PUT /decision`, the monitoring disposition path and any future caller answer 409. The confirmer must differ from the proposer by the fleet maker-checker primitive (`Proposal.approve`). The proposal writes no outbox row, so the rail, which acts only on `aml.case.status_changed.v1` with `newStatus` CLEARED or BLOCKED and `origin = PAYMENT_HOLD_INTAKE`, cannot release on it. A confirmation is written under a row lock and refused when the stored proposal differs from the one the confirmer read, so it cannot land on a replaced proposal. Both routes need `ROLE_COMPLIANCE` and a human staff token, because some realms give a service account that role. Residual: two accounts held by one person defeat the control; a pod built before this change still decides a hold case in one step, so the intake (`AML_PAYMENT_HELD_CONSUMER_ENABLED`, off by default) must be switched on only when every pod runs this build; the OPA grants for the two new actions are a separate governance change, and until it lands the routes are denied where `AUTHZ_ENFORCE=true`.

## Data

Monitoring tables hold party and account identifiers, amounts and decisions. Classification is `restricted`. Retention overrides erasure for these records (ADR-0118); the period is not asserted in this document.
